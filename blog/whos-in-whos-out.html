Always know who’s in, who’s out — and who they’re bringing

Ask any organizer what they actually want, and it’s not a fancier chat. It’s a straight answer to one question: how many people are showing up this time?

That number decides everything. How many courts to book. Whether to grab the big table or the small one. How much food to order. Whether tonight is even worth running. And in a group chat, that number is the single hardest thing to pin down.

GroupThat gives it to you at a glance — and then goes one better.

A live in / out list, every time

Every meetup has one screen that answers the whole question. A running count of who’s in, who’s out, and exactly which names are on each side. It updates the instant someone taps, so you’re never tallying replies or re-asking people who already answered.

No detective work. No “wait, did Sara say she was coming?” You open the app and you know.

The best part isn’t the reminder that goes out automatically. It’s never having to wonder who’s actually coming.

It counts the guests, too

Here’s the number that quietly wrecks a headcount: the plus-ones. Someone’s bringing a roommate. Two people are bringing their partners. A regular is introducing a friend who might join the group. In a chat, those extras get mentioned once and then forgotten — and suddenly you’re four short on chairs.

GroupThat folds guests right into the count. When a member is bringing someone, it shows up in the total, so the number you’re planning around is the real number walking through the door — not just the members who tapped “in.”

Why organizers love it

  • Book the right amount. Courts, tables, tickets, pizzas — size them to reality, including guests.
  • No surprises at the door. The count you saw this morning is the count that shows up.
  • Spot your regulars and your newcomers. Guests today are often members next month.

Mute until the next meetup

Visibility cuts the other way, too — toward the members. Not everyone makes it every week, and there’s nothing more annoying than a group blowing up your phone about a night you already said you’re skipping.

When someone’s out, GroupThat lets them mute until the next meetup. The chatter for this round goes quiet, and they rejoin automatically when the next invite comes around — no un-muting to remember, no risk of missing the plan next time.

For organizers, that’s a gift in disguise: people stay in the group instead of leaving over notification fatigue. The ones who are in get the logistics. The ones who are out get peace and quiet. Everybody comes back next week.

Only the right people get pinged — so nobody mutes the whole group forever and misses the plans that matter.

The takeaway

Running a recurring group is really an information problem: who’s coming, how many total, and who needs to hear what. GroupThat is built to answer exactly that — a live in/out count with guests included, and a mute that respects everyone’s attention.

Know your number. Bring the right amount. Keep the group happy. That’s the whole job — and it’s the job GroupThat is built to do.
